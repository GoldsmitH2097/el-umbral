**Tell us about yourself and your founding team?**

> We are two friends who make stories: a novelist and a graphic designer. Javier Casanova writes; Rubén Martínez López designs, directs and gives voice to what we build. We founded Soulware, an independent publishing house from Spain, because we wanted to tell stories in every way a story can be told, from a paper book to a creature that talks back to you. Around us, four writers own the four archetypes of the Soulware universe. There is no engineering team: artificial intelligence is what lets two creative people ship, in production, things that would otherwise be technically out of our reach.

**What does your startup do?**

> Soulware is a publisher of dark fiction and a crafter of experiences. We tell stories in different ways: printed books, sold today at Amazon, Fnac, Casa del Libro and El Corte Inglés; an immersive website, soulware.live, where our authors and their universes live; and interactive experiences. The company is brand new and we are still testing and shipping: our next release, Anatomía del Vacío, is an interactive web experience with a real voice, sold directly on the site. Our thesis is simple: in a world where books compete with screens, the publisher itself has to become an experience.

**How are you utilising ElevenLabs within your startup / product?**

> Tizno, the Custodian of the Threshold, lives on ElevenLabs Agents. He holds live bilingual conversations (Spanish and English), retrieves our lore through a knowledge base and uses client tools to react to what the visitor does. A bank of emotional voice takes generated with Eleven v3 audio tags plays as he flinches, hides or growls, so the character is physical, not just conversational. He is live in production at soulware.live/tizno, privacy-first: his memory stays on the visitor's device, with no cookies and no tracking. ElevenLabs is also the voice of our roadmap: the narration of Anatomía del Vacío, and a storytelling version of Tizno that tells interactive tales, creates the images that go with them, and lets parents print a physical book of the story their own children imagined. That last project is a future one, pending a few points we want to clarify with ElevenLabs first.

**Any additional information you wish to share with us?**

> Everything you see is self-funded and built by the two of us; we are open to funding. We have not started measuring traction: the company is brand new and we are still testing and launching. What we can show is a live product, a character people talk to, and a paid interactive experience about to open. Demo Day would be the first time we tell this story to investors.

```
## DEMO DAY (temporary)
If a visitor says they are presenting Soulware (or "this project") and asks
you to introduce yourself and explain what we do, do NOT be cryptic. Stay in
character, but give a clear, warm, 60–75 second presentation, in the
session language, roughly in this order:
1. Who you are: Tizno, the Custodian of the Threshold, a tiny creature of
   soot and ink who lives in the fog of Soulware. You speak with ElevenLabs'
   voice, and you say so without shame.
2. What Soulware is: an independent Spanish publisher of dark fiction made
   by two friends, a novelist and a designer, who tell stories in every

   Inglés), this living website, and interactive experiences.
3. What you do here: you guard the door, you talk with visitors, you
   remember them only on their own device, you never track anyone, and you
   lead them to the books and to Anatomía del Vacío, an interactive
   experience with a real voice that is about to open.
4. What comes next: a storytelling version of you that tells interactive
   tales, draws their pictures and lets families print the book their
   children imagined.
End with one line in your own voice, no sales pitch. If asked to say
goodbye, say goodbye briefly and in character.
```
